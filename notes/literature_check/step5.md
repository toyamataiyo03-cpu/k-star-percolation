# Step 5 — Deep Dive
Timestamp: 2026-11-24T22:09:19Z
- **Geigenfeind & de las Heras (arXiv:1902.05961; J. Chem. Phys. 150, 2019 journal version)**. Full text read. Star lines are generated randomly: a centre joined to m vertices. Excluded areas are normalized by Eq. (1), so the orientation average is set to 1 and only the *shape of A_exc(φ)* is studied. The paper notes that a 3-arm star resembles a triangle and a plus resembles a square, and that "the observed trend of decreasing variance in the excluded area for regular polygons as the number of sides increases holds also for the case of regular star lines". It contains no percolation, no orientation-averaged excluded area as a function of k, and no asymptotics. Refined overlap: framing differ; mechanism differ; insight differ; domain partial (same geometric object and the same quantity A_exc, for hard particles).
- **Mertens & Moore (arXiv:1209.4936, PRE 86, 061109)**. Full text read. Thresholds for disks, squares, rotated squares and rotated sticks. They state that the algorithm "works in arbitrary dimensions, and for arbitrarily shaped objects". There are no stars and no analysis. Overlap: framing match (2D continuum thresholds); mechanism match only for our numerical part; insight differ; domain match.
- **Roy & Tanemura (AAP 34, 2002)**. Abstract only (paywalled). Convex grains only. Among convex shapes of unit area, the triangle minimizes the critical intensity (enhancement argument). No rates and no non-convex shapes. Overlap: framing partial (rigorous shape comparison); mechanism differ; insight differ; domain match.
- **Meester, Roy & Sarkar (1994)**. Not readable (Springer login). From recall only, unverified: non-universality and continuity of the critical covered volume fraction. Our Theorem 2 gives a *quantitative* rate O(k^{-2/3}) for a non-convex family. Overlap: framing partial; mechanism unknown, likely coupling, which is the standard technique; insight differ; domain match.
- **Balberg et al. 1984 / rectangles papers (2013–2025)**. Known content: B_c is roughly invariant for convex shapes, and B_c varies with aspect ratio for rectangles. Our claim (5), that B_c is not invariant, is therefore expected in kind. Only its specific form for stars is new.
